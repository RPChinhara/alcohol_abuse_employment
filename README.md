# Causal Analysis of Alcohol Abuse on Employment 

This course project for EC 607 (Health Econometrics, IIT Bombay) investigates the causal effect of alcohol abuse on employment outcomes using econometric methods that correct for endogeneity. Using data from Terza (2002), encompassing 9,822 male individuals with detailed information on alcohol consumption, employment, demographics, family background, and state-level alcohol policies, we replicate and extend prior work through three causal modeling frameworks that also incorporate feature selection as well as strong instrumental variables. Exploratory analysis reveals consistent negative associations between employment and alcohol abuse, age, marital status, race, self reported health, and state-unemployment rate. Baseline linear probability, probit, and logit models indicate a modest negative association ( ≈ −2%) that decreases and becomes statistically insignificant after adjusting for confounders. However, addressing endogeneity through two-stage least squares (2SLS), bivariate probit, and two-stage residual inclusion approaches with parental alcoholism as instruments yields a markedly stronger and statistically significant effect across all the approaches, suggesting that the naive models substantially underestimate the true causal impact due to bias from reverse causality and omitted variables. These results highlight the importance of properly addressing endogeneity when estimating behavioral health effects in labor economics and imply that alcohol abuse exerts a large negative causal effect on employability.
